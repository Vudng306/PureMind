# Spaced retrieval practice and vocabulary retention in adult language learners

## Abstract

This study asked whether spacing short retrieval quizzes over several days helps adult learners keep new vocabulary better than reviewing the same words in one block. Eighty-four adult learners of Spanish at a community college took part. Learners in the spaced retrieval group recalled 62% of the target words after four weeks, compared with 48% in the massed review group and 41% in the re-reading group. The difference between the spaced and the massed groups was moderate in size (Cohen's d = 0.71).

## 1. Background

Many adult learners prepare for vocabulary tests by reviewing word lists the evening before. Laboratory work has shown that spreading practice over time and testing oneself instead of re-reading both improve long-term memory, but few studies have combined the two in an ordinary evening class with working adults. This study tested the combination in a real course, using the class's own word lists rather than invented words.

---

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Participants

The participants were 84 adults (52 women and 32 men) aged 23 to 61, enrolled in a beginner Spanish evening course. All had studied Spanish for less than one year. Three learners who missed more than two sessions were excluded before the analysis, leaving 81 learners whose results were analysed.

### 2.2 Design

Learners were randomly assigned to one of three conditions. In the spaced retrieval condition, learners answered a five-minute quiz on the new words at the start of three sessions spread over nine days. In the massed review condition, learners spent the same fifteen minutes reviewing the words in a single session. In the re-reading condition, learners re-read the word list with translations for fifteen minutes in one session.

### 2.3 Materials and measures

The target vocabulary was 60 Spanish nouns and verbs taken from the course textbook. Retention was measured with a written recall test four weeks after the last practice session: learners saw the English word and wrote the Spanish word. A response counted as correct when the spelling allowed only one reading of the word.

---

## 3. Results

After four weeks, the spaced retrieval group recalled 62% of the target words on average, the massed review group 48% and the re-reading group 41%. The spaced retrieval group did better than the massed review group (Cohen's d = 0.71) and better than the re-reading group (Cohen's d = 0.94). The massed review and re-reading groups did not differ reliably. Learners in the spaced group also reported, in a short questionnaire, that the quizzes felt harder than re-reading.

## 4. Discussion and limitations

The results suggest that a few short quizzes spread over a week and a half can be added to an evening language class at little cost and with a clear benefit for retention. The study has several limitations. It was run at a single college with one teacher, so the results may not hold for other teachers or settings. The retention test came four weeks after practice; longer delays were not tested. The word lists contained only nouns and verbs, and the test measured written recall only, not speaking.

## 5. Conclusion

Spacing short retrieval quizzes across sessions improved four-week vocabulary retention for adult beginners compared with massed review or re-reading of the same words.
